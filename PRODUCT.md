# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Fizzed is for casual soda drinkers, curious collectors, and friends who want a simple place to track, rate, and compare sodas. Users are browsing familiar bottles, regional finds, limited releases, imports, and novelty flavors, often looking for a specific drink quickly or keeping a lightweight personal diary of what they have tried.

## Product Purpose

Fizzed is a soda rating and logging app: a collector-heavy social catalog for sodas. It helps users browse a cleaner soda database, find drinks by name or brand, log ratings from 1 to 5, write short reviews, and build a profile around their taste. Success means users can find a soda without fighting messy product data, recognize drinks visually, and feel like each rating adds to a personal soda collection.

## Brand Personality

Minimal, playful, collector-ish.

Fizzed should feel like a fun, vibrant hobby app with enough polish to make browsing and logging feel satisfying. It should have personality without turning into a candy-store cartoon, and it should make the soda catalog feel collectible rather than administrative.

## Anti-references

Fizzed should not feel like a corporate SaaS dashboard, a generic database admin tool, a direct Letterboxd clone, or an AI-generated gradient interface. Avoid glassy blobs, purple-blue gradient styling, fake premium SaaS language, and over-serious productivity aesthetics. It should not look childish or chaotic; the fun should come from color, imagery, collection mechanics, and small interaction details.

## Design Principles

1. Put the soda first: names, images, flavors, and ratings should be immediately scannable.
2. Keep collecting lightweight: logging should feel fast, low-friction, and repeatable.
3. Make the catalog feel curated: reduce messy source-data artifacts and present sodas as recognizable entries.
4. Use personality through restraint: fun and vibrant, but not loud enough to get in the way of browsing.
5. Preserve trust in the data: make filters, search, and rating breakdowns predictable and easy to understand.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use standard production accessibility practices: readable contrast, keyboard-friendly controls, visible focus states, reduced-motion alternatives where motion is added, semantic labels for form controls, useful image alt text, and no meaning conveyed through color alone. Aim for WCAG AA contrast for body text and core controls.
